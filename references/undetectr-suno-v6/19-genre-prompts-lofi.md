## The Lo-Fi Prompt Formula

![The lo-fi prompt formula: substyle, BPM 70-90, drums and swing, instrument, vinyl crackle, mood](https://undetectr.com/blog/suno-prompts-for-lofi/formula.webp)

Every genre has a prompt structure, but lo-fi needs its own. A trap prompt cares about 808s and hi-hat rolls; a rock prompt lives on guitar tone. A lo-fi prompt lives or dies on *texture and feel* — the swing in the drums and the dust on the recording. Stack these six elements, in roughly this order, and you give Suno a target instead of a vibe:

- **Substyle** — be specific. "Chillhop study beats" not "lo-fi", "lo-fi jazz" not "jazzy", "lo-fi house" not "chill house". The substyle sets the whole sonic world and decides the tempo range.
- **Tempo** — use a real BPM, and keep it slow. The lo-fi heart is **70 to 90 BPM**. Study beats sit around 80, chillhop a little higher, lo-fi house jumps to ~112, ambient lo-fi drifts down toward 65.
- **Drum / swing feel** — this is the element lo-fi cannot skip. Ask for it loose and human: "swung boom-bap drums", "laid-back drums slightly behind the beat", "dusty soft kick and snare", "head-nodding groove". Straight, gridded drums are the fastest way to sound like AI.
- **Lead instrument** — name the centerpiece. Lo-fi leans on a small palette: "warm Rhodes electric piano", "mellow jazz guitar", "dusty upright piano", "muted trumpet", "soft vibraphone". One clear lead beats three competing ones.
- **Mood** — lo-fi is an emotional genre. Use evocative words: "nostalgic", "melancholic", "cozy", "rainy-day", "late-night", "bittersweet", "peaceful".
- **Lo-fi textures** — the multiplier. "Dusty vinyl crackle", "warm tape hiss", "analog tape saturation", "wow and flutter", "background room ambience", "aged cassette warmth". One or two of these turns a clean instrumental into actual lo-fi.

Put together, a complete lo-fi prompt looks like this:

Lo-fi hip-hop, 82 BPM, swung boom-bap drums slightly behind the beat, warm Rhodes electric piano, nostalgic and mellow, dusty vinyl crackle and tape hiss, no vocals

Notice what is doing the work: the substyle and tempo set the head-nod, the swing and crackle keep it from sounding sterile, and the "no vocals" keeps it a usable loop. Keep the total to roughly six or seven descriptors — fewer and the output drifts generic, more and Suno starts contradicting itself. The rest of this guide is that formula filled in for every major lo-fi style.

## Lo-Fi Tips: Crackle, Swing & Loopability

![Lo-fi prompt tips: add vinyl crackle and tape warmth, loose swung drums, keep it instrumental and loopable](https://undetectr.com/blog/suno-prompts-for-lofi/tips.webp)

The prompts above set the world. These details are how you push the result from "good AI lo-fi" to "sounds like a real tape."

### Layer the texture, do not rely on one word

"Vinyl crackle" alone gives you a clean track with crackle pasted on top. Stack two or three texture descriptors instead — "dusty vinyl crackle, warm tape hiss, background room ambience" — and Suno commits to the aesthetic across the whole mix. Add "wow and flutter" or "wobbly detuned" on the lead instrument for that slightly-warped cassette character that defines the genre.

### Make the drums swing, not march

The fastest AI tell in lo-fi is drums that sit perfectly on the grid. Real boom-bap breathes. Tell Suno to do the same: "swung drums," "laid-back and slightly behind the beat," "loose human groove," "head-nodding feel." One of those phrases does more for believability than any amount of chord detail. Straight, quantized drums are the single biggest reason an AI lo-fi loop sounds fake.

### Keep it instrumental and loopable

Suno adds vocals by default, and a stray vocal line ruins a study-beat loop. Put "no vocals" or "instrumental only" in every lo-fi prompt. For loops that sit in the background, also ask for a "minimal arrangement," "repeating 4-bar loop," and "no big build or drop." Lo-fi is meant to be low-event — resist the urge to stack dramatic descriptors that make the track demand attention. If you do want a soft vocal over a slow, warm groove, our [Suno R&B prompts](./22-genre-prompts-r-and-b.md) are a better starting point.

### Fight the "too clean" AI tell

Suno defaults toward bright and polished, and polish is exactly what makes AI lo-fi sound fake — and what detectors lock onto. Counter it: ask for "warm," "dusty," "muffled," "low-passed," "aged cassette." Avoid stacking pristine, crisp, crystal-clear, hi-fi, which all push toward the sterile sound. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the best Suno prompt formula for lo-fi?

Lo-fi prompts work best when you stack six things: substyle, a slow tempo (70 to 90 BPM is the heart of the genre), a loose drum/swing feel, the lead instrument (Rhodes, jazz guitar, dusty piano), the mood, and the lo-fi textures themselves (vinyl crackle, tape hiss, room ambience). For example: chillhop study beats, 82 BPM, swung boom-bap drums, warm Rhodes chords, nostalgic and mellow, dusty vinyl crackle, no vocals. The textures and the swing are what separate real lo-fi from a clean instrumental, so never leave them out.

### How do I keep Suno lo-fi tracks instrumental and loopable?

Add 'no vocals' or 'instrumental only' to the style field, because Suno adds vocals by default and a stray vocal line ruins a study-beat loop. For loopability, keep the prompt simple and the arrangement static: ask for a 'repeating 4-bar loop', 'minimal arrangement', and 'no big build or drop'. Lo-fi is meant to sit in the background, so resist the urge to stack dramatic descriptors. A calm, even, low-event track loops cleanly and works for playlists.

### What BPM should lo-fi tracks be in Suno?

The lo-fi sweet spot is 70 to 90 BPM. Classic lo-fi hip-hop and study beats sit around 75 to 85 BPM, chillhop a touch higher near 85 to 90, lo-fi house jumps to roughly 110 to 118 because it is dance-rooted, and ambient lo-fi can drift down to 60 to 70. Always give Suno a real number rather than 'slow' — the BPM anchors the swing and the head-nod feel that define the genre.

### How do I get the vinyl crackle and tape warmth sound?

Name it directly in the style prompt. Suno responds to texture words: 'dusty vinyl crackle', 'warm tape hiss', 'analog tape saturation', 'wow and flutter', 'aged cassette warmth', 'background room ambience'. Pair those with a slightly detuned or wobbly lead like 'wobbly out-of-tune Rhodes' and a soft, filtered low-pass feel. Those textures are the entire aesthetic of lo-fi, so they belong in almost every prompt on this page.

### Does AI lo-fi get flagged by Spotify and distributors?

Yes. Soft drums and vinyl crackle do not hide AI origin. Streaming platforms and distributors screen the audio at intake for spectral fingerprints, mathematically precise timing, metadata tags, an unnaturally clean noise floor, and uniform dynamic range, and those signatures are present in lo-fi just like any other genre. Spotify said in September 2025 that it had removed more than 75 million spammy tracks in the previous twelve months, and Deezer said in July 2026 that AI-generated tracks had passed half of its daily uploads. Lo-fi is a favorite for passive-income playlists, which makes the screening risk higher, not lower, so process the finished file before you distribute it.
