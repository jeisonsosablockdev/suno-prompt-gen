## The Hip-Hop Prompt Formula

![The hip-hop prompt formula: subgenre, BPM, drum character, 808 and bass, mood, vocal flow](https://undetectr.com/blog/suno-prompts-for-hip-hop/formula.webp)

Every prompt below is built from the same seven slots. Fill them in and you will get a focused beat instead of a mush. Skip a slot and Suno fills it randomly.

#### The Formula

[Subgenre] + [BPM] + [Drum Character] + [808 / Bass] + [Mood] + [Vocal Flow] + [Reference]

- **Subgenre** — be precise: "trap soul", "boom-bap", "UK drill", "lo-fi hip-hop", "melodic rap". This is the single most important slot.
- **BPM** — always a number. "140 BPM" reads cleaner than "fast". Half-time trap can be written as "140 BPM, half-time feel".
- **Drum Character** — "crisp triplet hi-hat rolls", "dusty boom-bap kick and snare", "syncopated drill hats".
- **808 / Bass** — "booming sub 808", "sliding 808 glides", "warm round bass". Name it or lose it.
- **Mood** — "dark and menacing", "nostalgic and reflective", "woozy and introspective".
- **Vocal Flow** — "confident male rap", "melodic auto-tuned vocals", "laid-back storytelling flow", or "no vocals" for an instrumental.
- **Reference** — an era or sound anchor: "modern Atlanta", "1990s New York", "Brooklyn drill". Keep it about sound, not a specific living artist's name where you can avoid it.

**Worked example:**

Trap, 142 BPM half-time feel, crisp triplet hi-hat rolls and punchy clap, booming distorted 808 with glides, dark and confident, assertive male rap with sparse ad-libs, modern Atlanta sound

That is the entire toolkit. Everything that follows is just this formula filled in for each sub-style, ready to paste into Suno's Custom Mode style field.

## Hip-Hop Specific Tips

![Hip-hop prompt tips: name the 808 and drums, use meta-tags, call ad-libs, avoid AI tells](https://undetectr.com/blog/suno-prompts-for-hip-hop/tips.webp)

### Match the BPM to the subgenre

The single fastest way to get a generic beat is the wrong tempo. Trap and drill should be written at their fast count with a half-time feel (140 BPM that *knocks* at 70), boom-bap holds the 85–95 pocket, lo-fi drops to 70–85, and melodic rap floats in the middle. Put the number in every prompt.

### Use meta-tags for structure

In the lyrics field, structure tags drive the arrangement: [Intro], [Verse], [Hook], [Bridge], [Outro]. Hip-hop is hook-driven, so make the [Hook] repetitive and short — Suno delivers a repeated phrase with far more conviction than four different lines. A clear hook is the difference between a beat and a song. Pop leans on the hook even harder; our [Suno pop prompts](./21-genre-prompts-pop.md) add a [Pre-Chorus] so the chorus lifts.

### Ad-libs: less is more

Drop ad-libs on their own short lines in parentheses next to the bars: (yeah), (uh), (skrrt), (gang). They give the call-and-response energy real rap has. But stack too many and the vocal render turns to mud. Two or three per section is plenty. For full call-and-response between a lead and a choir, our [Suno gospel prompts](./17-genre-prompts-gospel.md) show how to tag it in the lyrics field.

### Always name the drums and the 808

Suno will not give you a knocking low end or crisp triplet rolls unless you ask. "Booming 808", "sliding 808", "triplet hi-hat rolls", "dusty boom-bap kick" — these phrases do the heavy lifting. A prompt without explicit drum language defaults to a thin, polite mix.

### Avoiding the AI tells

Two giveaways mark AI rap: vocals that drift slightly off the grid, and a hi-hat pattern that is *too* perfectly even. Counter the first by keeping lyrics conversational and evenly metered; counter the second by asking for "syncopated" or "swung" hats rather than straight ones. And generate 5–10 variations — flow quality swings hard between takes, so audition several and keep the one that sits in the pocket. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### What BPM should I use for hip-hop in Suno?

It depends on the subgenre. Trap sits around 130 to 150 BPM (the beat is often felt at half time, so it reads slower than it counts). Boom-bap lives at 85 to 95 BPM. UK drill runs 138 to 145 BPM with a sliding 808, and NY drill is similar but slightly busier. Lo-fi hip-hop sits at 70 to 85 BPM, and melodic or emo rap usually lands between 75 and 95 BPM. Always put the number in the prompt — Suno responds far better to '140 BPM' than to 'medium tempo'.

### Do meta-tags and ad-libs work in Suno for hip-hop?

Yes. Structure tags like [Intro], [Verse], [Hook], [Bridge], and [Outro] in the lyrics field control the arrangement, and you can drop ad-libs in parentheses on their own short lines — (yeah), (skrrt), (uh), (gang) — to get the call-and-response feel rappers use. Keep ad-libs sparse. Stacking too many makes the vocal render muddy.

### How do I get a hard 808 in a Suno trap beat?

Name it explicitly. Use phrases like 'heavy distorted 808 bass', 'sliding 808 glides', or 'booming sub 808' in the style field. Pair it with a drum descriptor such as 'crisp triplet hi-hat rolls' and 'punchy clap on the snare'. Suno will not reliably produce a knocking low end unless you ask for it directly — vague prompts default to a thin, mixed-down bass.

### Can Suno do UK drill and NY drill differently?

It can, if you steer it. For UK drill, ask for a 'sliding 808 bassline, dark eerie piano or bell melody, syncopated drill hi-hats, 142 BPM'. For NY drill, ask for the same sliding 808 but 'aggressive, sample-driven, faster hat patterns, menacing energy'. Adding a regional cue like 'UK drill' or 'Brooklyn drill' as a genre anchor helps Suno pick the right pocket.

### Why do my Suno rap vocals sound robotic or off-beat?

Two common causes. First, your lyrics are too dense or too poetic — Suno renders conversational, evenly-metered bars far more cleanly than complex internal-rhyme schemes. Keep lines to 6 to 12 syllables. Second, you have not processed the track. Even strong generations carry AI timing and spectral tells.
