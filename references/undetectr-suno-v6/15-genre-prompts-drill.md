## What Makes Drill Sound Like Drill

![Drill sub-styles: UK drill, NY/Brooklyn drill, sample drill, melodic drill, Jersey-club drill](https://undetectr.com/blog/suno-prompts-for-drill/subgenres.webp)

Before the prompts, it helps to know exactly which elements you are steering toward, because Suno will quietly drop every one of them if you do not name it. Four things separate drill from regular trap:

- **The sliding 808.** This is the signature. Instead of a static sub holding one note, the 808 glides — pitch-bends — from note to note, often sliding down at the end of a phrase. That slide is the sound people mean when they say "drill bass." If your 808 is not sliding, it is not drill.
- **Dark, minor melodies.** Drill melodies are cold and eerie: a lone piano line, a haunting bell, a tense string motif, almost always in a minor key. Think menacing and sparse, not bright or busy. The space around the melody matters as much as the notes.
- **~140 BPM with a half-time feel.** Drill counts fast (around 140 BPM) but feels slow because the snare lands on the 3 instead of the 2 and 4. It knocks like a 70 BPM beat while the hats move at the full count. Write the fast number and add "half-time feel."
- **Syncopated hi-hat triplets.** Drill hats are busy and syncopated — rapid triplet rolls and off-grid placements rather than the straight, even pattern of standard trap. The slight unevenness is intentional and is part of why drill grooves the way it does.

Every prompt in this guide is just those four ingredients, plus a vocal direction and a regional anchor, arranged in a consistent order so Suno reads them cleanly.

## The Drill Prompt Formula

![The drill prompt formula: substyle, BPM around 140, sliding 808, dark melody, hi-hat pattern, vocal flow](https://undetectr.com/blog/suno-prompts-for-drill/formula.webp)

Fill in these six slots and you get a focused drill beat instead of a mush. Skip a slot and Suno fills it randomly — usually by defaulting to generic trap.

#### The Formula

[Drill Substyle] + [BPM] + [Sliding 808] + [Dark Minor Melody] + [Hi-Hat Pattern] + [Vocal Flow]

- **Drill Substyle** — be precise: "UK drill", "Brooklyn drill", "sample drill", "melodic drill", "Jersey-club drill". This is the single most important slot and the regional anchor that steers everything else.
- **BPM** — always a number, and add the feel: "142 BPM, half-time feel". The number does most of the work.
- **Sliding 808** — the non-negotiable slot. "Sliding 808 bassline with glides", "gliding 808 between notes", "pitch-bending 808 slides". Name it or you lose the genre.
- **Dark Minor Melody** — "dark eerie piano melody", "haunting bell line", "ominous minor strings". Keep it cold, sparse, and menacing.
- **Hi-Hat Pattern** — "syncopated drill hi-hats", "busy off-grid triplet rolls", "sparse snappy drill drums".
- **Vocal Flow** — "confident male rap", "deep-voiced menacing flow", "melodic auto-tuned vocals", or "no vocals" for an instrumental.

**Worked example:**

UK drill, 142 BPM half-time feel, sliding 808 bassline with glides, dark eerie piano melody, syncopated drill hi-hats, confident deep-voiced male rap, cold menacing London sound

## Drill-Specific Tips

![Drill prompt tips: describe the sliding 808, dark minor melodies, syncopated hi-hat triplets, keep lyrics within guidelines](https://undetectr.com/blog/suno-prompts-for-drill/tips.webp)

### Describe the 808 slide, do not just say "808"

This is the difference between drill and trap. Writing "heavy 808" gives you a static sub. Writing "sliding 808 bassline with glides" or "pitch-bending 808 between notes" gives you the drill low end. If you want the classic downward slide at the end of a phrase, say "808 with downward slides" explicitly. Suno will not infer the glide — it is the one phrase you should never drop.

### Anchor the melody in a dark minor key

Drill melodies are cold, sparse, and eerie. Reference the instrument and the mood together: "dark eerie piano melody", "haunting bell line", "ominous minor strings", "lonely music-box motif." Avoid "bright" or "uplifting" — those pull Suno toward pop-trap. The negative space around the melody is part of the sound, so "sparse" is a useful word here.

### Ask for syncopated, off-grid hats

Standard trap hats are even; drill hats are busy and syncopated, with rapid triplet rolls placed slightly off the grid. Use "syncopated drill hi-hats", "busy off-grid triplet rolls", or "snappy drill drums." The slight unevenness is what makes drill groove, and it also doubles as one of your best defenses against the "too perfect" AI tell (more on that below).

### Write the fast BPM, feel the half-time

Drill counts at ~140 BPM but the snare lands to make it feel like 70. Always write the fast number and add "half-time feel" so Suno places the snare correctly. Get the tempo wrong and even a perfect 808 will not save the beat — it is the most common reason a drill prompt comes back sounding off.

### Use meta-tags and keep ad-libs sparse

In the lyrics field, structure tags drive the arrangement: [Intro], [Verse], [Hook], [Outro]. Drill is hook-driven, so make the [Hook] short and repetitive — Suno delivers a repeated phrase with far more conviction than four different lines. Drop ad-libs on their own short lines, two or three per section at most; stacking them turns the vocal render to mud. Keep lyric content within Suno's usage guidelines and your distributor's — the genre's themes are fine, but explicit threats or targeting can get a track rejected at intake. For line-length and rhyme guidance that keeps the flow on beat, see [writing lyrics for Suno AI](./25-technique-write-lyrics-for-suno-ai.md).

## Avoiding the AI Tells

Drill exposes two AI giveaways more than most genres. The first is a hi-hat pattern that is *too* perfectly even — real drill hats breathe and shift, so always ask for "syncopated" or "off-grid" hats rather than straight ones, and audition several takes because the hat feel swings hard between generations. The second is a vocal flow that drifts slightly off the grid; counter it by keeping lyrics conversational and evenly metered, six to twelve syllables a line, rather than dense internal-rhyme schemes the model struggles to place.

Generate 5–10 variations and keep the one that sits in the pocket — flow and 808 quality both swing between takes, so the first render is rarely the best. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### What BPM is drill in Suno?

Drill mostly lives around 140 BPM, but it is felt at half time, so it reads slower than it counts — a 140 BPM drill beat knocks like a 70 BPM track. UK drill runs 138 to 145 BPM, NY and Brooklyn drill sit at the same count but feel busier, and Jersey-club drill pushes faster toward 130 to 145 with a bouncier kick pattern. Always put the number in the prompt. Suno responds far better to '142 BPM, half-time feel' than to 'medium tempo'.

### How do I get a sliding 808 in Suno?

Name it directly. Phrases like 'sliding 808 bassline with glides', 'gliding 808 between notes', or 'pitch-bending 808 slides' are what produce the signature drill low end. The slide — the 808 bending from one note to the next instead of sitting still — is the single defining feature of drill. A vague prompt gives you a static sub that sounds like generic trap, so spell the glide out every time.

### What is the difference between UK drill and NY drill prompts?

Both share the sliding 808 and syncopated hats, but the mood and drums differ. For UK drill, ask for a 'dark eerie piano or bell melody, sparse snappy drums, cold and menacing' at 138 to 142 BPM. For NY or Brooklyn drill, keep the slide but request 'busy aggressive hi-hat patterns, sampled vocal chops, sample-driven and hype' at 142 to 145 BPM. Adding the regional anchor — 'UK drill' or 'Brooklyn drill' — helps Suno pick the right pocket.

### Can Suno do Jersey-club drill?

Yes, if you steer it. Jersey-club drill fuses the sliding 808 and dark melody of drill with the bouncy bed-squeak kick pattern and triplet bounce of Jersey club. Ask for a 'Jersey-club drill, bouncy syncopated kick pattern, bed-squeak sample, sliding 808, fast bounce' around 135 to 145 BPM. It is one of the newer crossover sounds, so be explicit — Suno needs both genre cues to blend them.

### Why do my drill beats sound like generic trap?

Almost always because the 808 is not sliding and the hats are too even. Drill lives or dies on the gliding 808 and the syncopated, slightly off-grid hi-hat triplets — if you do not name both, Suno defaults to a static sub and straight hats, which reads as plain trap. Add 'sliding 808 with glides' and 'syncopated drill hats' to every prompt, and lean on a dark minor melody to lock the genre in.
