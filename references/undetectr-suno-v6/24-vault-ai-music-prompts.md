## Why Prompts Matter More Than the AI Model

Here is a truth that surprises most new AI music creators: **the same AI model can produce both terrible and excellent music, depending entirely on the prompt.** Suno V5 with a vague prompt will produce worse results than Suno V4 with a great prompt. The model improvements between versions are real, but they are marginal compared to the difference between a bad prompt and a good one.

Think of it this way. When you type "pop song" into Suno, the AI has to make thousands of decisions on its own: What tempo? What key? What instruments? What vocal style? What song structure? What production aesthetic? What mood? Each of those decisions defaults to whatever the model considers "average" for pop — and the result sounds average.

When you type "upbeat synth-pop, 122 BPM, female vocals, breathy and layered, analog synthesizer pads, arpeggiated bass line, punchy electronic kicks, shimmering hi-hats, 80s-inspired production, euphoric chorus, polished mix" — you have made most of those decisions yourself. The AI focuses its generation power on executing your specific vision rather than guessing at what you want.

The Prompts Vault exists because writing prompts like that requires knowing the vocabulary. What are "arpeggiated bass lines"? What is the difference between "warm" and "bright" production? What vocal textures exist beyond "male" and "female"? The vault gives you that vocabulary organized by genre, with tested combinations that work.

## Anatomy of a Perfect AI Music Prompt

![Anatomy of an AI music prompt: genre, tempo, mood, vocal style, instrumentation, production, and era layers](https://undetectr.com/blog/ai-music-prompts-vault/prompt-anatomy.webp)

Every effective AI music prompt contains the same core elements, regardless of genre. Understanding this anatomy lets you build, modify, and combine prompts with confidence.

### Element 1: Genre and Subgenre (Most Important)

The genre tag is the single most impactful element. It sets the foundation for everything else. Always use the most specific subgenre available: "melodic dubstep" over "EDM," "neo-soul" over "R&B," "math rock" over "rock." The more specific the genre, the more focused the output.

### Element 2: Tempo (BPM)

Specifying tempo locks in the energy level. Lo-fi works at 70-85 BPM. Pop sits at 100-130 BPM. Drum and bass lives at 170-180 BPM. Getting this wrong throws off the entire generation. The vault includes the optimal BPM range for every genre.

### Element 3: Mood and Energy

Mood descriptors shape the emotional character: melancholic, euphoric, aggressive, dreamy, triumphant, haunting, playful, intense. Use 1-2 mood tags per prompt. More than that creates conflicting signals.

### Element 4: Vocal Style

Vocal descriptions are where most manual prompts fail. "Female vocals" is almost useless. "Breathy female vocals with subtle vibrato and reverb tail" is specific enough to produce consistent results. The vault includes detailed vocal texture descriptors for every genre.

### Element 5: Instrumentation

List the 3-5 most important instruments with their characteristics: "detuned analog synthesizer pads," "fingerpicked acoustic guitar," "heavy 808 sub-bass," "brushed jazz drums." Instrument tags with adjectives outperform bare instrument names every time.

### Element 6: Production Style

How the track should sound from a mixing/mastering perspective: "lo-fi tape hiss," "clean and polished," "heavy compression," "wide stereo field," "vintage warmth." Production tags affect the overall texture and feel.

### Element 7: Era or Influence

Optional but powerful: "90s boom-bap influence," "2010s blog house," "70s progressive rock," "modern bedroom pop." Era tags anchor the sound in a specific aesthetic tradition.

The rule of thumb from our [Suno prompts guide](./29-technique-general-prompts-guide.md): **use 4-8 style tags total, most important first.** Every prompt in the vault follows this principle.

## The Complete Metatag Guide

![Metatag reference chart of standard and genre-specific song structure tags for Suno Custom Mode](https://undetectr.com/blog/ai-music-prompts-vault/metatag-guide.webp)

Metatags are structural markers you place in the lyrics field of Suno's Custom Mode. They control song structure — telling the AI when to start a verse, switch to a chorus, insert an instrumental break, or build to a bridge. Without metatags, the AI decides structure on its own, and the results are often meandering or repetitive.

### Standard Structure Tags

- **[Verse]** — marks the beginning of a verse section
- **[Chorus]** — marks the chorus; AI typically increases energy and adds vocal layers
- **[Pre-Chorus]** — a building section before the chorus; creates anticipation
- **[Bridge]** — a contrasting section, usually appearing once; different melody and energy
- **[Outro]** — the ending section; AI typically reduces energy and fades
- **[Instrumental Break]** — an instrumental section with no vocals
- **[Hook]** — a short, catchy melodic phrase; often repeated
- **[Intro]** — the opening section before the first verse

### Genre-Specific Structure Tags

- **[Drop]** — for EDM/electronic; marks the high-energy drop section after a build
- **[Build]** — rising energy section leading to a drop or chorus
- **[Breakdown]** — stripped-back section, common in electronic and rock
- **[Spoken]** — spoken-word delivery rather than singing
- **[Rap]** — rap delivery; useful for genre-blending tracks
- **[Ad-lib]** — background vocal ad-libs and shouts

The vault includes recommended metatag structures for every genre. A pop song might use [Intro] → [Verse] → [Pre-Chorus] → [Chorus] → [Verse] → [Pre-Chorus] → [Chorus] → [Bridge] → [Chorus] → [Outro]. An EDM track would use [Intro] → [Build] → [Drop] → [Breakdown] → [Build] → [Drop] → [Outro]. Using the right structure for your genre is as important as the style tags.

## Advanced Metatags: Audio Quality and Vocal Textures

Beyond standard structure tags, the vault includes advanced metatags that give you granular control over audio characteristics. These are the tags that separate amateur AI music from professional-sounding output.

### Audio Quality Tags

These tags influence the overall sonic quality of the generation:

- **[dynamic_range:wide]** — produces output with more contrast between quiet and loud sections, sounding more natural and less compressed
- **[clean_master]** — targets a clean, clear master with minimal distortion and balanced frequency response
- **[warm_analog]** — adds warmth and analog character to the production
- **[crisp_digital]** — clean, precise digital production aesthetic
- **[lo-fi_texture]** — intentional degradation for lo-fi genres (tape hiss, vinyl crackle, bit reduction)
- **[spatial_wide]** — wide stereo image with elements spread across the stereo field

### Vocal Texture Tags

These tags control vocal delivery and timbre with precision:

- **[Sultry]** — smooth, seductive vocal delivery common in R&B and jazz
- **[Breathy]** — airy, intimate vocal quality with audible breath
- **[Falsetto]** — high-register vocal technique, lighter than full voice
- **[Gritty]** — rough, textured vocal quality with natural distortion
- **[Ethereal]** — floaty, otherworldly vocal character with reverb
- **[Powerful]** — full-bodied, belted vocal delivery with strong projection
- **[Whispered]** — quiet, intimate, spoken-adjacent vocal delivery
- **[Raspy]** — textured, slightly hoarse vocal quality

### Delivery Style Tags

These control how the vocal performance is delivered:

- **[Staccato]** — short, punchy vocal phrasing
- **[Legato]** — smooth, connected vocal phrasing
- **[Syncopated]** — off-beat rhythmic vocal delivery
- **[Layered Harmonies]** — multiple vocal layers singing in harmony
- **[Call and Response]** — alternating vocal phrases, common in gospel and blues
- **[Rhythmic Chanting]** — repetitive rhythmic vocal patterns

Not all of these tags work equally well on every platform. The vault specifies which tags are most effective on Suno V5 specifically, since that is where metatags have the most impact. Some tags are more experimental and may require multiple generations to produce the desired effect.

## The 7 Rules of Effective AI Music Prompts

These rules apply to every prompt in the vault and should guide any prompt you write or modify:

### Rule 1: Most Important Tags First

AI generators weight the beginning of your prompt more heavily than the end. Put genre, subgenre, and the most critical sonic characteristic first. Mood, tempo, and instrumentation follow. Production details go last.

### Rule 2: 4-8 Style Tags Maximum

More tags does not mean better results. Past 8 tags, the AI starts losing focus and producing generic output as it tries to satisfy too many constraints simultaneously. The sweet spot is 4-8 well-chosen, specific tags.

### Rule 3: Specific Beats Vague Every Time

"Breathy female vocals with subtle vibrato" will always outperform "female vocals." "Detuned analog synthesizer pads" will always outperform "synth." Adjectives and modifiers are not filler — they are instructions.

### Rule 4: Use Subgenres, Not Parent Genres

"Melodic dubstep" is better than "EDM." "Neo-soul" is better than "R&B." "Shoegaze" is better than "rock." Parent genres are too broad for the AI to produce focused results.

### Rule 5: Include One Mood Tag

Always include at least one emotional descriptor: melancholic, euphoric, aggressive, dreamy, triumphant, haunting, playful, intense. Mood shapes the entire generation's character. Two mood tags work if they are complementary (e.g., "melancholic, introspective"). Avoid contradictory moods.

### Rule 6: Match Tempo to Genre

Every genre has a natural BPM range. Lo-fi at 150 BPM does not work. Drum and bass at 80 BPM does not work. If you specify tempo, make sure it matches your genre. The vault includes the correct BPM range for every genre and subgenre.

### Rule 7: Structure Tags Are Not Optional

Using [Verse], [Chorus], [Bridge] and other metatags in the lyrics field is the single most impactful thing you can do beyond the style prompt. Without structure tags, AI generators tend to produce repetitive, structureless output. With them, you get songs that have actual dynamics and progression.

## How to Combine and Customize Vault Prompts

The vault prompts are starting points, not final products. The most interesting AI music comes from combining elements across genres and modifying prompts to create something new. Here are proven techniques:

### Genre Fusion

Take the instrumentation from one genre's prompt and the vocal style from another. Jazz instrumentation with hip-hop vocal delivery. Electronic production with country lyrics and acoustic elements. Rock guitar tones with R&B vocal harmonies. The vault gives you the accurate vocabulary for both genres, so your fusion prompt is specific enough to produce coherent results.

### Mood Swapping

Take a complete genre prompt and swap the mood tags. A pop prompt built for "euphoric, energetic" output becomes something entirely different when you replace those with "melancholic, nostalgic." Same instruments, same tempo, completely different emotional character.

### Era Blending

Combine a modern genre prompt with production tags from a different era. Modern trap beats with 90s boom-bap production warmth. Contemporary pop with 70s analog warmth and tape saturation. This creates sounds that feel familiar but fresh.

### Vocal Texture Experiments

Swap the vocal texture tags between genres. Put [Ethereal] vocals on a metal track. Put [Gritty] vocals on a pop ballad. Put [Whispered] vocals on an EDM track. Unexpected vocal choices often produce the most distinctive results.
