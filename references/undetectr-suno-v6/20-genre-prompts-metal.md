## The Metal Prompt Formula

![The metal prompt formula: subgenre, tempo, guitar tone and tuning, rhythm, vocal style, production](https://undetectr.com/blog/suno-prompts-for-metal/formula.webp)

Every genre has a prompt structure, but metal needs descriptors that pop and rock simply do not. A [pop prompt](./21-genre-prompts-pop.md) never mentions tuning; a metal prompt lives or dies on it. Stack these six elements, in roughly this order, and you give Suno a target instead of a vibe:

- **Subgenre** — be specific. "Melodic death metal" not "death metal", "Bay Area thrash" not "thrash", "djent metalcore" not "heavy". The subgenre sets the whole sonic world, and it tells Suno how harsh the vocals should be.
- **Tempo** — use a real BPM. Metal spans a huge range: a crushing 70 BPM doom dirge and a 200 BPM thrash burner are both metal, and the number tells Suno which one you mean.
- **Guitar tone & tuning** — the element metal cannot skip. Name the tuning and the attack: "down-tuned drop-C palm-muted guitars", "tight chugging drop-B riffs", "thick fuzzed-out down-tuned guitars", "razor-sharp tremolo-picked guitars". Tuning is what makes it heavy.
- **Rhythm feel** — this is how Suno tells the subgenres apart. "Blast beats", "relentless double-kick", "galloping thrash drums", "syncopated groove", "slow lumbering half-time". Name the drum pattern, not just "heavy drums".
- **Vocal style** — a hard fork in metal. "Harsh guttural growl", "high black-metal shriek", "metalcore scream with clean choruses", or "powerful clean operatic vocals". Decide whether you want harsh, clean, or both, and say so.
- **Production** — the finisher. "Modern polished metal production", "raw lo-fi underground recording", "massive wall-of-sound mix", "dry and brutal". This aligns the mix density and how much the low end dominates.

Put together, a complete metal prompt looks like this:

Melodic death metal, 170 BPM, down-tuned drop-C palm-muted guitars, blast beats and relentless double-kick, harsh growled vocals with soaring clean choruses, modern polished production

Notice what is doing the work: the subgenre and tuning set the weight, the rhythm descriptor locks the subgenre in, and the vocal fork decides whether you get growls, screams, or clean singing. Keep the total to roughly six or seven descriptors — fewer and the output drifts toward generic distorted rock, more and Suno starts contradicting itself and the mix turns to mush. The rest of this guide is that formula filled in for every major metal style.

## Metal-Specific Tips: Growls, Blast Beats & Solos

![Metal prompt tips: specify vocal style, double-kick and blast beats, call guitar solos and breakdowns](https://undetectr.com/blog/suno-prompts-for-metal/tips.webp)

The prompts above set the world. These details are how you push the result from "good AI metal" to "sounds like a real band."

### Call growls and screams explicitly — and expect resistance

Suno can do harsh vocals, but it leans toward cleaning them up. Name the exact style in the style field, "harsh guttural growl," "high black-metal shriek," "metalcore scream" — and reinforce it with a meta-tag in the lyrics field where the delivery changes.

[Verse] [Harsh Vocals] ... [Chorus] [Clean Vocals] ...

Be realistic: Suno often **sanitises** the most extreme vocals, pulling a guttural growl back toward a raspy shout, so the purest brutal-death or raw-black-metal vocals may take several generations to land — or never fully land. Subgenres where harsh-but-intelligible vocals are the norm, metalcore, melodic death, are far more reliable.

### Spell out the rhythm, not just "heavy drums"

Blast beats, double-kick, and galloping rhythms are the spine of metal, and Suno will not produce them unless you ask by name. "Relentless double-kick," "fast blast beats," "galloping thrash drums," "slow lumbering half-time" — each one steers the whole feel. This is the single descriptor that most often decides whether the track sounds like metal or like distorted rock.

### Get solos and breakdowns with meta-tags

A shred solo and a breakdown are the two things the style field reliably fails to deliver on its own. Both live in the **lyrics** field. Drop the tag exactly where you want it, usually after the second chorus for a solo, and Suno hands that section over instead of singing.

[Chorus] ... [Guitar Solo] ... [Breakdown] ... [Verse 3] ...

Steer the tone in the style field too — "sweep-picked shred solo" or "slow syncopated chugging breakdown" — because pairing the in-lyric tag with a style descriptor is far more dependable than either alone. The same [Breakdown] tag drives electronic tracks too: our [Suno EDM prompts](./16-genre-prompts-edm.md) pair it with [Build] and [Drop].

### Make it heavy with tuning, and fight the "too clean" tell

Heaviness lives in the low end. Name a tuning — drop-D, drop-C, down-tuned to B — and ask for "thick chugging palm-muted" riffs. At the same time, Suno's default polish is exactly what makes AI metal sound fake and what detectors lock onto. Counter it with "raw," "dry and brutal," or "underground production," and avoid stacking pristine, crystal-clear, flawless. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the best Suno prompt formula for metal?

Metal prompts work best when you stack six things in order: subgenre, tempo, guitar tone with tuning, rhythm feel, vocal style, and production. A complete example is: melodic death metal, 170 BPM, down-tuned drop-C palm-muted guitars, blast beats and double-kick, harsh growled vocals with clean choruses, modern polished production. The tuning and the rhythm feel do the heaviest lifting here, because drop tuning and blast beats are what separate metal from hard rock in Suno's output. A vague prompt like metal song gives you generic distortion with no weight; a stacked one gives you something that actually chugs.

### How do I get growls and screams in a Suno metal track?

Name the vocal style explicitly in the style field and reinforce it with meta-tags in the lyrics field. Use descriptors like harsh growled vocals, guttural death growl, high-pitched black metal shriek, or metalcore scream. Then drop a tag such as [Growl] or [Harsh Vocals] in the lyrics where you want that delivery. Be aware that Suno often sanitises the most extreme vocals, pulling a guttural growl back toward a raspy shout, so you may need several generations to land a convincing one, or lean on subgenres where harsh-but-intelligible vocals are the norm.

### Why does my AI metal track sound weak and not heavy?

Weak output usually means the prompt skipped tuning and rhythm. Heaviness in metal comes from down-tuned guitars and the low end, not just from the word heavy. Add drop-D, drop-C, or down-tuned to B, plus palm-muted chugging and tight double-kick. Ask for a thick guitar tone and a punchy modern mix. Also avoid contradicting yourself: pairing brutal with clean and bright fights itself. Pick a lane, name the tuning, and let the rhythm descriptors carry the weight.

### Can I get guitar solos and breakdowns in Suno metal tracks?

Yes, and the reliable way is structure meta-tags in the lyrics field, not the style prompt alone. Drop [Guitar Solo] where you want a lead, usually after the second chorus, and [Breakdown] where you want the track to drop into a slow, heavy, palm-muted section. Reinforce the tone in the style field with phrases like sweep-picked shred solo or syncopated chugging breakdown. Pairing the in-lyric tag with the style descriptor is far more dependable than hoping the style field produces either on its own.

### Do AI metal tracks get flagged by Spotify and distributors?

Yes. Heavy distortion and blast beats do nothing to hide AI origin. Streaming platforms and distributors screen the audio itself at intake for spectral fingerprints, mathematically precise timing, metadata tags, an unnaturally clean noise floor, and uniform dynamic range. Those signatures sit in a metal track exactly as they do in any other genre. Spotify said in September 2025 that it had removed more than 75 million spammy tracks in the previous twelve months, and Deezer said in July 2026 that AI-generated tracks had passed half of its daily uploads. A crushing metal prompt does not change what the audio carries. Process the finished file before you distribute it.

### Which metal subgenres does Suno handle best?

Suno is strongest on subgenres where the vocals are harsh-but-intelligible and the production is modern, such as metalcore, melodic death metal, and groove metal. It is weaker on the extremes, true guttural brutal death metal, raw lo-fi black metal, where it tends to sanitise the vocals and clean up the intended ugliness. Doom and stoner metal also come out well because the slow, fuzzy, riff-driven sound plays to Suno's strengths. Start with a subgenre in its comfort zone, then push toward the extremes once your prompt structure is dialled in.
