## What Is the Best Suno Prompt Formula for Rock?

**Six slots, in this order: subgenre and era, tempo, guitar tone, drum feel, vocal character, production.** Every genre has a prompt structure, but rock needs its own. A trap prompt cares about 808s and hi-hat rolls; a rock prompt lives or dies on guitar tone and how human the drums feel.

![The rock prompt formula: subgenre, tempo, guitar tone, drum feel, vocal character, production era](https://undetectr.com/blog/suno-prompts-for-rock/formula.webp)

- **Subgenre** — be specific. "Garage rock" not "rock", "90s grunge" not "alternative", "Southern hard rock" not "heavy". The subgenre sets the whole sonic world.
- **Tempo** — use a real BPM. Rock has a wide range: a brooding 78 BPM ballad and a 180 BPM punk burner are both rock, and the number tells Suno which.
- **Guitar tone and distortion**: the element rock cannot skip. Name the character: "crunchy overdriven guitars through a cranked British-style amp", "jangly clean 12-string", "thick fuzz-pedal riffs", "chugging palm-muted distortion".
- **Drum feel** — tell Suno to loosen up. "Loose human groove", "pocket drumming slightly behind the beat", "driving four-on-the-floor with live room sound". This is your single best weapon against the gridded AI sound.
- **Vocal character** — rock vocals need attitude. "Raspy belted male vocals", "snotty bratty punk delivery", "smoky bluesy female vocals", "detached deadpan baritone".
- **Production and era**: the multiplier. "1970s analog tape warmth", "raw 90s lo-fi recording", "modern arena production". One phrase here aligns the tone, drums and vocal treatment at once. Describe the era rather than naming a band; see [why](#bands).

Put together, a complete rock prompt looks like this:

1970s hard rock, 128 BPM, thick crunchy overdriven guitars, loose human drum groove with room ambience, raspy belted male vocals, warm analog tape production, heavy blues-rock swagger

Notice what is doing the work: the subgenre and era set the world, the guitar and drum descriptors stop it from sounding sterile, and the vocal grit keeps it from sounding polite. Keep the total to roughly six or seven descriptors: fewer and the output drifts generic, more and Suno starts contradicting itself. The rest of this guide is that formula filled in for every major rock style.

## Suno AI Prompt Guide for Hard Rock

**To prompt hard rock in Suno, name the era, give a BPM between roughly 100 and 140, describe the gain, ask for a live drum groove and a belted vocal.** Hard rock pushes the gain and the attitude without crossing into metal, so keep the drums big but human and let the riff carry the song.

### Step by step

1. **Pick the era or subgenre.** "1970s hard rock", "modern hard rock", "stoner rock", "80s glam". This one phrase sets the amp sound, the drum room and the vocal treatment.
2. **Give a BPM.** The hard rock prompts below run from a 104 BPM stomp to a 144 BPM drive. "Fast" means different things to the model on different takes; a number does not.
3. **Describe the gain, not a brand.** "Thick crunchy overdriven guitars", "chugging palm-muted riff", "dual harmonised lead guitars", "down-tuned fuzz".
4. **Keep the drums human.** "Pounding groove drums", "live room drums", "drummer pushing the chorus". On V6 this matters more: heavy-genre users report drums turning electronic.
5. **Describe the voice.** "Raspy belted", "gritty commanding", "soaring high". Delivery, not just gender.
6. **Mark the solo in the lyrics.** Put `[Guitar Solo]` after the second chorus and name the solo tone in the style field.

## Do These Rock Prompts Work on Suno V6?

**Yes, with three additions.** The prompts on this page were written on Suno V5. As of 9 September 2026 Suno runs three V6 models, v6, v6-wild and v6-mini, and every earlier model, including V5 and v5.5, has been retired. v6 and v6-wild need Pro or Premier; free accounts get v6-mini. - **Add an arrangement sentence.** The most common V6 rock complaint is a strong intro and an empty verse. The full method, with section cues, is in our [V6 rock prompts guide](./04-v6-rock-prompts.md).

## Can You Use Band Names in Suno Prompts?

**Not reliably. Suno’s help centre says it restricts prompts that include “the names of well known artists or people”, and that such songs may not generate.** The same page lists copyrighted or trademarked terms. So "in the style of" a named band is the one rock shortcut that can stop a generation outright.

Describe the sound instead. An era plus a tone plus a feel carries most of what a band name was doing:

- Instead of a 70s guitar hero: "1970s blues-rock, heavy riffs with expressive string bends, cranked vintage amp, loose thundering drums".
- Instead of a nu-metal band: "late-90s nu metal, down-tuned seven-string chug, hip-hop groove drums, whispered verses into shouted choruses".
- Instead of a 90s alt-rock band: "90s alternative rock, loud-quiet dynamics, clean verses, crunchy chorus, angsty vocals".

Every prompt on this page now follows that rule. If you have an older library of prompts that name bands, rewriting the reference as era and tone is the first fix to try when a generation fails.

## Rock-Specific Tips: Solos, Grit & Live Feel

![Rock prompt tips: call guitar solos with meta-tags, ask for human drums, add vocal grit, beat the too-clean tell](https://undetectr.com/blog/suno-prompts-for-rock/tips.webp)

The prompts above set the world. These details are how you push the result from "good AI rock" to "sounds like a band."

### How do I get a guitar solo in Suno?

Put a structure tag in the **lyrics** field exactly where you want the solo, usually after the second chorus. Suno hands that section to the lead instead of the vocal.

[Chorus] ... [Guitar Solo] [Verse 3] ...

Steer the tone by naming it in the style prompt too: "soaring bluesy lead guitar solo" or "fast palm-muted shred solo." Pairing the in-lyric tag with a style descriptor is far more reliable than either alone. If you want rock energy under jazz harmony, see the [jazz fusion prompts](./12-genre-prompts-jazz.md) in our jazz guide, which uses the same solo tags for sax and piano.

### Make the drums human

The fastest AI tell in rock is drums that are too perfect. Real drummers push and pull against the click. Tell Suno to do the same: "loose drum groove," "slightly behind the beat," "live room drums," "human pocket feel." One of these phrases does more for believability than any amount of guitar detail.

### Put grit in the vocals

Rock vocals are not supposed to be clean. Use descriptors that add texture and attitude: raspy, gravelly, snotty, belted, smoky, cracking at the edges, desperate. Specify the delivery, not just the gender. A "raspy belted male vocal" reads as rock; a "smooth male vocal" reads as a pop ballad wearing a leather jacket. For lyrics that carry that attitude, see our guide on [writing lyrics for Suno AI](./25-technique-write-lyrics-for-suno-ai.md). If you want a choir behind the last chorus, our [Suno gospel prompts](./17-genre-prompts-gospel.md) show how to tag lead and choir lines in the lyrics field.

### Fight the "too clean" AI tell

Suno defaults toward polish, and polish is what makes AI rock sound fake. Counter it: ask for "raw and unpolished," "analog tape warmth," "room-mic ambience," "live-off-the-floor." Avoid stacking pristine, crystal-clear and flawless, which all push toward the sterile, gridded sound. If V6 still sounds too clean for your taste, our [Suno V6 review](./09-v6-reactions-models-comparison.md) covers where users found it strongest and weakest by genre, and our roundup of [Suno alternatives](https://undetectr.com/blog/suno-alternatives) covers other generators.

Describe the amp character instead of writing generic distortion, and avoid stacking pristine, polished and crystal clear, which push the output toward a sterile, gridded sound.

### Do AI rock tracks get flagged by Spotify and distributors?

They can. Distorted guitars and live-sounding drums do not change what a file carries. A raw Suno export includes a 'made with suno' comment tag with the clip id, and V6-era WAV exports also carry a C2PA content-credentials manifest, which is metadata that says where the file came from. Distributors also screen the audio itself. A good prompt does not change any of that, so deal with the file before you distribute it. We do not publish pass rates for any platform.

### Should I write my own lyrics or let Suno generate them?

For rock, write your own. Generated lyrics tend toward generic, and rock vocals carry attitude that vague lyrics flatten. Keep lines short and conversational, lean on repetition in the chorus so Suno belts the hook, and use structure tags so verses, choruses and the solo land where you want them.
