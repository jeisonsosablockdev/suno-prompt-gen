## The EDM Prompt Formula

![The EDM prompt formula: subgenre, BPM, synth and lead, drop structure, energy, reference](https://undetectr.com/blog/suno-prompts-for-edm/formula.webp)

The general Suno formula is genre + tempo + mood + instruments + vocals + era + reference. For EDM, the priorities shift. Two elements that are optional elsewhere become mandatory here: **an explicit BPM** and **an explicit drop structure**. Without them, Suno produces a loop, not a track. The EDM order I use:

[Subgenre] + [BPM] + [Synth / Lead Character] + [Drop Structure] + [Energy] + [Reference]

Each slot does a specific job:

- **Subgenre** — be precise. "Deep house" and "tech house" sound nothing alike. "Riddim" is not "dubstep." Specificity here sets the entire palette.
- **BPM** — a hard number. This is the single most impactful EDM descriptor. It controls groove, build length, and where the drop lands.
- **Synth / Lead Character** — the signature sound: "detuned supersaw," "growling reese bass," "plucky analog stab," "screaming acid 303." This is the hook's timbre.
- **Drop Structure** — describe the arc: "8-bar build with riser and snare roll into a heavy drop," "breakdown then a euphoric second drop." This is what most people forget.
- **Energy** — the emotional target: "euphoric," "dark and driving," "peak-time," "hypnotic," "festival main-stage."
- **Reference** — anchor to a producer or label sound. "In the style of deadmau5," "Anjunadeep vibe," "Monstercat-style." Suno reads these as strong style anchors.

Put together: *"Progressive house, 126 BPM, warm plucked synth lead and rolling bassline, long emotional build into a soaring drop, euphoric and uplifting, in the style of Lane 8 and Anjunadeep."* That prompt tells Suno the tempo, the timbre, the shape, the feeling, and the lane. Compare it to "make a house song" and the gap is obvious.

## BPM, Builds, and Drop Meta-Tags

![EDM build and drop meta-tags arranged on a song timeline](https://undetectr.com/blog/suno-prompts-for-edm/meta-tags.webp)

A quick BPM cheat sheet, because getting this wrong is the most common reason an EDM prompt feels off:

- **Deep / tech house:** 120–126 BPM
- **Progressive house:** 124–128 BPM
- **Big-room / festival:** 126–132 BPM
- **Techno:** 128–135 BPM
- **Trance:** 132–140 BPM
- **Future bass:** 140–160 BPM (half-time feel, so it grooves at ~75)
- **Dubstep / riddim:** 140–150 BPM (half-time, drop hits on the 70–75 feel)

For the build and the drop, the style prompt only goes so far. When you want real control over where the energy moves, drop these **meta-tags** into the lyrics or structure field, even on an instrumental:

[Intro] [Build] [Drop] [Breakdown] [Build] [Second Drop] [Outro]

Suno treats these like section markers. **[Build]** ramps tension (risers, snare rolls, filter sweeps), **[Drop]** releases it, and **[Breakdown]** strips back to give the second drop somewhere to climb from. If you only remember one thing from this section: an EDM prompt without an explicit drop instruction will almost never produce a satisfying drop. For deeper vocal and structure-tag mechanics, the [main prompt guide](./29-technique-general-prompts-guide.md) covers it; here we're focused on the EDM-specific moves. Leaving out the tempo and leaving out structure tags both appear on our list of [common Suno AI issues](./27-technique-10-suno-ai-mistakes.md), each with a fix.

One more EDM-specific tip: decide **instrumental vs vocal up front**. If you want a pure club instrumental, add "instrumental, no vocals" or Suno will often add an unwanted topline. If you want vocal EDM, place the vocal: "female topline in the breakdown, vocal chops in the drop" beats a vague "with vocals" every time.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the best Suno prompt formula for EDM?

Use this order: subgenre + BPM + synth/lead character + drop structure + energy + reference. For example: "Future bass, 150 BPM, detuned supersaw lead, build into a vocal-chop drop, euphoric festival energy, in the style of Flume and San Holo." The BPM and drop-structure cues matter far more for EDM than for other genres because they tell Suno where the build, the breakdown, and the drop land. Without a stated BPM and a drop instruction, Suno tends to produce a flat loop with no payoff.

### What BPM should I use for each EDM subgenre in Suno?

Deep and tech house sit around 120 to 126 BPM, progressive house 124 to 128, big-room and festival 126 to 132, future bass 140 to 160 (half-time feel), trance 132 to 140, and dubstep/riddim 140 to 150 (half-time so the drop hits on the 70 to 75 BPM feel). State the number explicitly. "Medium tempo" gives Suno nothing; "128 BPM" anchors the whole arrangement.

### How do I get Suno to actually produce a drop?

Two things. First, describe the structure in the style prompt: "8-bar build with riser and snare roll into a heavy drop." Second, if you are using lyrics or a structure field, add meta-tags like [Build], [Drop], [Breakdown], and [Bridge] so Suno knows where the energy shifts. EDM is defined by tension and release, and Suno will not infer the release unless you ask for it directly.

### Should EDM tracks be instrumental or have vocals in Suno?

Both work, but they need different prompts. For instrumental drops, add "instrumental, no vocals" or you will get an unwanted topline. For vocal EDM, specify the vocal placement: "female topline in the breakdown, vocal chops in the drop" reads far better than a generic "with vocals." Future bass and progressive house lean vocal; techno and riddim lean instrumental.

### Why do my Suno EDM drops sound thin or fake?

Two common causes. The prompt is under-specified (no synth character, no layering cue), so Suno renders a single thin lead instead of a stacked drop. Or the drop has the tell-tale AI artifacts that real listeners and platform detectors both pick up on: smeared transients, an over-clean noise floor, and a too-perfect stereo image. Fix the first with richer descriptors ("layered supersaw stack, sub bass, gated reverb"). The second is a post-processing problem, not a prompt problem.

### Will my AI-generated EDM track pass Spotify and distributor checks?

Not by default. Spotify said in September 2025 that it had removed more than 75 million spammy tracks in the previous twelve months, Deezer said in July 2026 that AI-generated tracks had passed half of its daily uploads, and distributors run their own detection at the upload gate. AI-generated EDM is especially exposed because synthetic drops carry recognizable spectral and timing fingerprints. Process the exported track first to clear those signals before you distribute. That is the difference between a track that gets algorithmic surfacing and one that quietly never does.
