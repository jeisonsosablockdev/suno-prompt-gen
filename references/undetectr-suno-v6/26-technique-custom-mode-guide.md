## Why Custom Mode Changes Everything

![Simple Mode single input versus Custom Mode separate Style and Lyrics fields](https://undetectr.com/blog/suno-ai-custom-mode-guide/simple-vs-custom-mode.webp)

Simple Mode is a black box. You type a description and Suno decides everything: what genre it interprets, what lyrics to write, how to structure the song, what vocal style to use. Sometimes it nails it. Often it does not.

Custom Mode gives you **two separate input fields**:

- **Style field** — where you define genre, tempo, mood, instruments, vocal style, and sonic references
- **Lyrics field** — where you write the actual words the vocals sing, with structure tags that define song sections

This separation means you can write targeted lyrics while independently controlling the musical style. A melancholic folk song with happy lyrics. A dark trap beat with introspective poetry. Combinations that Simple Mode would never produce because it tries to match lyrics to style automatically.

The practical result: **Custom Mode tracks are more intentional, more dynamic, and more professional**. If you have been frustrated by inconsistent Simple Mode output, Custom Mode is the fix.

## How to Turn On Custom Mode

Suno’s help centre gives the route: go to **suno.com/create**, or click the Create icon in the left side toolbar, then **choose Custom from the options at the top**. Simple is the other option in the same place. Once Custom is selected you get the separate fields below, including a space to enter your own lyrics. The model picker sits in the top-right corner of the same Create form, and your choice stays set until you change it.

## Every Custom Mode Field, Explained

Most searches that land on this page ask the same thing in different words, and in several languages: what does each box in Custom Mode actually do? Here is every field and control, in the order you meet them, with Suno’s own description where it has published one.

### Lyrics field

Where you type the words Suno sings, with a structure tag such as [Verse] or [Chorus] at the top of each section. Suno’s help centre says that, whichever version you use, you retain ownership and rights to any original lyrics you input. Detail on writing for AI vocals is in [the Lyrics field section](#lyrics-field) and the tags are in [Structure Tags](#structure-tags) below.

### Style field (“Style of Music”)

The description of the sound: genre, era, tempo, the voice, the instruments and the mood. Many guides and searches still call it the Style of Music field; Suno’s current help articles call it the Style field. Suno’s slider documentation refers to it as your “style input”, and it is the text the Style Influence and Variety controls act on. How to write it is covered in [The Style Field](#style-field) below.

### Exclude

Click **Advanced Options** and the menu starts with Exclude. Suno’s instruction is to “enter any information (instruments, etc) that you do not want in your track.” Use it for categories you want kept out, such as drums, strings or backing vocals, rather than writing “no drums” in the Style field, where the word itself can pull drums in.

### Vocal Gender

Also under Advanced Options. Suno’s help centre says to select Male or Female there, and to define the voice further with terms like “gritty” or “soft” in the Style field, for example “blues rock with gritty male vocal” with Male selected.

### Weirdness, Style Influence and Audio Influence

Suno calls these Creative Sliders. **Weirdness** “goes from Safe to Chaos, where 50% is the normal expected result.” **Style Influence** “lets you choose how close you stay to your style input from Loose to Strong.” **Audio Influence** only appears when you use an audio upload. Typing “weirdness 20%” into the Style field does not move a slider; it is just more words for the model to read.

### Instrumental tracks

For a track with no vocals, say so in the Style field (“instrumental, no vocals”) and leave the Lyrics field empty or use only section tags. There is more on this under [Advanced Custom Mode Techniques](#advanced).

## The Suno V6 Controls: Variety, Max Mode and Models

Suno V6 launched on 9 September 2026 and, per Suno’s V6 FAQ, “all models prior to v6 have been retired.” Your old songs stay in your library, and you can still listen to, share, remaster and cover them, but every new generation in Custom Mode now runs on a V6 model. Three things in the form are new or changed.

### Variety: why V6 rewrites your Style field

Suno’s FAQ: the Variety slider “is designed to introduce variety in your outputs by adjusting and updating your style prompts. If you’d like to retain full control of your style tags, reduce the Variety slider to 0.” If a Style field you re-used came back rewritten, or the song came out different from last time, this is the reason. Set Variety to 0 when you want the text obeyed, and raise it when a prompt feels stale and you want new takes on it. Our guide to [Variety, Style Influence and Weirdness](./01-v6-changing-your-prompt-sliders.md) has a five-run worksheet for learning how the controls behave on your own prompt.

### Max Mode

Max Mode is not a slider. Suno describes it as “an option you can turn on for any generation when you want v6 to spend more on getting it right.” It costs more credits and is best for songs longer than two minutes, covers that should stay close to the original, transferring one song’s style onto another, and keeping vocals and style consistent through the whole track. For quick ideas and shorter songs, standard mode is all you need.

## The Style Field: Defining Your Sound

The Style field is where you write your [prompt](./29-technique-general-prompts-guide.md) — the description of the music's sound. Use the same 7-element formula:

#### Style Field Formula

[Genre] + [Tempo] + [Mood] + [Instruments] + [Vocal Style] + [Era] + [Reference]

**Example styles:**

#### Indie Folk Style

Indie folk, 92 BPM, intimate and melancholic, fingerpicked acoustic guitar, soft banjo, gentle strings, whispered male vocals, 2010s indie, influenced by Bon Iver

#### Dark Electronic Style

Dark electronic, 128 BPM, tense and cinematic, distorted synth bass, atmospheric pads, industrial percussion, no vocals, influenced by Perturbator and Carpenter Brut

#### R&B Style

Modern R&B, 85 BPM, sultry and atmospheric, warm synth pads, 808 bass, minimal hi-hats, smooth falsetto male vocals, late night intimacy, influenced by The Weeknd

Keep the Style field to **4 to 7 descriptors**. More than that and the model gets confused. Each descriptor should control a distinct dimension — do not repeat the same idea in different words.

**On V6:** the short descriptor list above was written for the retired models. Suno says V6 “understands more of the language and building blocks musicians use,” and it follows the Style field more literally, so anything you leave out, it decides for you. For songs you intend to keep, a longer Style field that says what each instrument and the singer do in each section works better. Paste-ready examples are in the [Suno V6 prompts guide](./00-suno-v6-prompts-guide.md).

## The Lyrics Field: Writing for AI Vocals

The Lyrics field accepts your written words plus structure tags. Suno will sing exactly what you write, using the vocal style defined in the Style field.

Tips for lyrics that Suno renders well (for a deep dive, see our full guide on [writing lyrics for Suno AI](./25-technique-write-lyrics-for-suno-ai.md)):

- **Write conversationally.** Simple, clear language renders more clearly than complex poetry. "I miss you every night" sounds better than "In nocturnal abyssal depths of longing I reside."
- **Keep lines short.** 6 to 10 syllables per line is the sweet spot. Long lines get rushed or truncated.
- **Use rhyming patterns.** ABAB or AABB rhyme schemes give Suno rhythmic anchors that improve melodic output.
- **Repeat chorus lines.** Repetition in the chorus makes Suno deliver the hook with more conviction and a stronger melody.
- **Use common words.** Unusual vocabulary or made-up words often come out garbled. Save exotic language for written poetry — Suno wants clarity.

## Structure Tags: Building a Real Song

![Suno structure tags from Intro to Fade Out with energy dynamics per section](https://undetectr.com/blog/suno-ai-custom-mode-guide/structure-tags-anatomy.webp)

Structure tags are the secret weapon of Custom Mode. They tell Suno how to organize your song into sections with different energy levels, dynamics, and musical characteristics.

- **[Intro]** — instrumental opening that sets the mood. Typically 10 to 15 seconds.
- **[Verse]** — the storytelling section. Lower energy than the chorus. Where narrative develops.
- **[Pre-Chorus]** — builds tension before the chorus. Energy rises. Creates anticipation.
- **[Chorus]** — the hook. Highest energy. Most memorable melody. The part listeners remember.
- **[Bridge]** — contrasting section that breaks the verse-chorus pattern. Different melody, different energy. Adds variety.
- **[Instrumental Break]** — a section with no vocals. Solo, breakdown, or atmospheric moment.
- **[Outro]** — closing section. Energy winds down. Resolves the song.
- **[Fade Out]** — gradual volume decrease. Classic ending technique.

#### Complete Song Structure Example

[Intro]

[Verse 1]
Walking down these empty streets at midnight
Every shadow holds a memory of you
The city sleeps but I am wide awake tonight
Wondering if you feel it too

[Pre-Chorus]
And I know I should let go
But my heart keeps holding on

[Chorus]
We are falling, falling, falling
Through the spaces in between
We are calling, calling, calling
For a love we have never seen

[Verse 2]
Morning comes and washes all the neon out
Coffee cups and conversations that we had
I replay them over trying to figure out
How something good could turn so bad

[Chorus]
We are falling, falling, falling
Through the spaces in between

[Bridge]
Maybe in another life we get it right
Maybe in another world we hold on tight

[Chorus]
We are falling, falling, falling

[Outro]

Without structure tags, Suno produces a monotone wall of sound. With them, you get a dynamic song with contrast, build-up, release, and a memorable hook. **Always use structure tags for any track you plan to keep.**

On V6, treat tags as strong hints rather than commands. Suno does not publish a tag specification, and the same tag can behave differently between generations. They land most reliably when the Style field describes the same arrangement, so the model reads the instruction twice. For intros that hum and endings that vamp or cut off, see the [V6 intro and ending guide](./06-v6-intro-outro-prompts.md).

## Advanced Custom Mode Techniques

### Instrumental Tracks

For tracks without vocals, write "no vocals" or "instrumental only" in the Style field and leave the Lyrics field empty (or use only structure tags like [Intro], [Build], [Drop], [Breakdown], [Outro]). This produces cleaner instrumental output than trying to suppress vocals after the fact.

### Multiple Vocal Styles

You can indicate vocal changes within the lyrics using parenthetical directions: "(whispered)", "(belted)", "(spoken word)". Suno does not always follow these perfectly, but it often adjusts delivery based on these cues.

### Extending a Song

If your generated track is too short or you want to add more sections, use Suno's "Extend" feature: click the triple-dot icon next to the song, open Remix/Edit and choose Extend, then pick where to extend from. When you have an extension you like, Suno's "Get Whole Song" stitches it to the original. V6 can also edit one section or a single lyric in plain language, such as "change the chorus so it's sung by a gospel choir", instead of regenerating the song. This continues the song in the same style, allowing you to add additional verses, bridges, or an extended outro. For more advanced editing, [Suno Studio](https://undetectr.com/blog/suno-studio-guide) gives you stem-level control and Warp Markers for precise adjustments.

### Genre Blending

Custom Mode excels at hybrid genres. Instead of a single genre, combine two: "jazz-infused trip hop", "folk-electronic fusion", "cinematic hip hop". The Style field handles these combinations better than Simple Mode because you can be more precise about which elements come from which genre.

## The Complete Custom Mode Workflow

![Seven-step Custom Mode workflow from concept to distribution through Undetectr](https://undetectr.com/blog/suno-ai-custom-mode-guide/custom-mode-workflow.webp)

1. **Concept**: decide on genre, mood, and topic before touching Suno
2. **Write lyrics**: craft your words with structure tags. Keep it conversational. Use rhyming patterns.
3. **Write the style prompt and set the controls**: genre, tempo, mood, instruments, vocals, era. Then pick the model, set Variety to 0 if you want the Style field obeyed, and fill Exclude with anything you do not want.
4. **Generate and iterate, one change at a time**: every generation gives you two takes. Change one thing between runs: a tempo, a mood word, a vocal descriptor or a slider, so you know what made the difference.
5. **Select the best take, then download once**: listen in Suno before downloading. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between Simple Mode and Custom Mode on Suno?

Simple Mode takes a single description and Suno decides the rest: lyrics, style and structure. Custom Mode splits the input into separate fields: a Lyrics field for your own words with structure tags like [Verse] and [Chorus], a Style field for genre, voice, instruments and mood, and Advanced Options such as Exclude, Vocal Gender, Weirdness and Style Influence. Custom Mode gives you far more control over what comes out.

### What goes in the Style of Music field in Suno Custom Mode?

The Style field (often called Style of Music) describes the sound, not the words: genre, era, tempo, the voice, the instruments and the mood. On Suno V6, which follows the style text more literally, it works best written as instructions for each part, for example what the drums do in the verse and chorus, rather than a string of adjectives. Things you do not want go in the Exclude field, not here.

### Can I write my own lyrics in Custom Mode?

Yes, that is the main reason to use it. Suno's help centre says to choose Custom at the top of the Create page, where you get a space to enter your own lyrics, and that you retain ownership and rights to any original lyrics you input. Suno sings your words with the voice you describe in the Style field.

### How do I turn on Custom Mode in Suno?

Go to suno.com/create, or click the Create icon in the left side toolbar, then choose Custom from the options at the top. The Lyrics and Style fields and the Advanced Options appear. The model picker is in the top-right corner of the same Create form.

### When should you switch from Simple Mode to Custom Mode?

When you want to write your own lyrics or choose the music style yourself. Custom Mode is not needed to download an MP3, to make an instrumental or to browse trending songs. For anything you intend to keep, release or sell, Custom Mode is the better starting point because every field is under your control.

### What is the Exclude field in Suno Custom Mode?

Exclude sits at the top of Advanced Options. Suno describes it as where you enter any information, such as instruments, that you do not want in your track. List categories like drums, strings or backing vocals there instead of writing no drums in the Style field, where the word drums can pull them in.

### Which model should I use in Custom Mode: v6, v6-wild or v6-mini?

Suno retired every model before V6, so these are the three choices, in the model picker at the top right of the Create form. v6 is the flagship for control and precision, v6-wild is less predictable and built for exploration, and v6-mini is the faster model available on every plan including Free. v6 and v6-wild need Pro or Premier.

### Why does Suno V6 change what I typed in the Style field?

That is the Variety slider. Suno's V6 FAQ says it adds variety by adjusting and updating your style prompts, and that you should reduce it to 0 to retain full control of your style tags. Style Influence is a different control: it sets how closely the song follows the style text, from Loose to Strong.

### What is Max Mode in Suno V6?

An option you can turn on for any generation when you want V6 to spend more on getting it right. Per Suno's FAQ it costs more credits and is best for songs longer than two minutes, covers that should stay close to the original, transferring one song's style onto another, and keeping vocals and style consistent through the whole track. For quick ideas and shorter songs, standard mode is enough.

### What structure tags can I use in Custom Mode lyrics?

The common ones are [Intro], [Verse], [Pre-Chorus], [Chorus], [Bridge], [Instrumental Break], [Outro] and [Fade Out]. They tell Suno how to organise the song into sections with different dynamics. On V6 they work as strong hints rather than commands, so they land most reliably when the Style field describes the same arrangement.

### Can I choose a male or female singer in Custom Mode?

Yes. In Custom Mode, open Advanced Options and set Vocal Gender to Male or Female, then describe the voice further in the Style field with words like gritty or soft, per Suno's help centre.
